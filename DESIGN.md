# PathProof design

A student and club organizer sit together at a library desk in daylight, checking a borrowing form on a laptop before publishing it. The surface should feel like a working diagram on warm paper: light, quiet, and legible.

Use warm ivory canvas, near-white paper, dark plum ink, burnt orange for active paths, and muted green for successful terminations. Orange is an action and evidence color; green is semantic. No gradients, glows, decorative charts, or generic metric cards. Use a local system sans with a monospace evidence voice. Compact logo, generous page title, dense but orderly workspace. Navigation is top-level and real: Inspect and Edit model. The topology occupies the largest surface, beside a finding list. Evidence sits below the graph. Inputs and selected paths must remain readable on narrow screens.

Nodes have small numbered headers and distinct terminal indicators. Orange paths correspond to actual selected edge IDs. Show a textual equivalent of the selected path. Error and status copy explains the next action. Native controls, visible keyboard focus, semantic headings, 44px primary targets. Motion is limited to 120ms press feedback; nothing moves to attract attention. Reduced-motion preference removes transforms. Colors use OKLCH tokens. Diagram can scroll horizontally inside its container at small widths; the page itself cannot.

Original utility layout: no fitting complete catalog blueprint identified by parent research. CSS is direct and focused; a component framework is unnecessary for the small native-control surface.
